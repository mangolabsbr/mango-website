Almost every restaurant we talked to this year had the same menu problem, and it was never about the food. The menu lived in a PDF someone's nephew made in 2019, or on a laminated card that goes stale the week the fish supplier changes. Moving one price means paying a designer, or reprinting forty sheets.

So we built [Menoodo](/apps/menoodo), and it's live now. You build the menu once in a dashboard, and guests open it by scanning a QR code on the table. Nothing to install, nothing to update by hand.

## From paper to phone in an afternoon

![The three setup steps on the Menoodo home page: bring your menu, make it yours, share one link](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/setup.webp)

The part that usually kills these projects is the typing. So you don't type: hand over a PDF, a spreadsheet or the address of your current website, and the AI import hands back an editable menu with dishes, descriptions, prices and categories already in place. What's left is fixing the two dishes it misread, not entering sixty. After that it's drag-and-drop — categories in the order you actually serve them.

## It should look like your restaurant, not like us

![The theming section: four restaurant presets on the left and a live phone preview of the Trattoria Fiorella menu on the right](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/theming.webp)

Start from a preset, then take it apart — your colors, your fonts, your logo, your cover photos. The four menus on our home page are the same product with four owners' choices, and the phone shows the real thing, live.

I think this is the part that decides whether a digital menu survives past the first month. If it looks like a generic template, the printed card genuinely feels warmer, and the QR code quietly stops getting used.

## In the language your guest actually reads

![The same demo menu side by side in English and Portuguese, with the language flag in the header](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/menu-languages.webp)

Enable the languages your room hears. Auto-translate drafts the gaps and you approve every word before it ships — a machine guess about an allergen isn't something to publish unread. There are around 120 languages available for the menu itself, right-to-left included, and the dashboard is translated into eight.

## A QR code you'd actually put on a table

![The free Menoodo QR generator with a dark green code, a print preview and SVG and PNG export buttons](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/qr.webp)

The QR designer takes your colors and drops your logo in the center, exports SVG or PNG for the print shop, and previews the code on an A6 table tent at real proportions so you can judge it before the printer does. It's also free to use without an account, over at [the QR generator](https://www.menoodo.com/tools/qr/qr-code-generator) — your link is turned into a code in your own browser, and never reaches us.

## A menu that changes during the day

![The Menoodo feature grid: AI import, languages, QR codes, scheduling, multiple restaurants and privacy](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/features.webp)

Menus are not static documents, which is exactly why paper loses. Lunch specials appear at lunch and leave in the afternoon on their own. A dish that ran out stays on the menu, marked honestly, instead of disappearing and reappearing. Dietary tags sit next to the dishes that need them. And if you run more than one place, they live in one account, each with its own menu, theme and link.

## Your guests tell you first

![The reviews panel with a 4.8 average and three recent diner reviews](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/reviews.webp)

Diners can rate the visit from the menu they already have open — no account, no trip to an app store. Those reviews land in your dashboard inbox and nowhere else. It won't stop anyone writing a public review, but it does mean the wine-list complaint reaches you on the night, while you can still send something to the table.

## And the menu tells you what happened

![The analytics section with menu views, unique devices, a most-viewed-dishes chart and a breakdown of languages guests read in](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/analytics.webp)

Which dishes get read, what time people browse, which languages they switch to. That last one has been the surprise in our own testing — the split between languages rarely matches what an owner expects, and it's the one thing a paper menu could never tell you.

## Measured with a "no" that works

![The consent prompt at the bottom of a menu, asking whether views can be counted, with accept and decline buttons](/articles/menoodo-digital-menus-for-restaurants/menu-privacy.webp)

Those numbers come with a real question attached. A diner is asked before anything is counted, declining is a normal outcome rather than a maze, and there are no ad trackers or third-party cookies on the menu at all. Everything is aggregate, diner data is hosted in the EU, and none of it is sold or shared. A menu is a strange place to be tracked, and we'd rather have thinner charts than pretend otherwise.
